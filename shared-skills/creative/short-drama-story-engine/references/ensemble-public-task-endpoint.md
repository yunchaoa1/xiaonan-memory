# Ensemble Public-Task Endpoint

## Purpose

Use this reference when an 8–12 character ensemble must converge on one public task without having formed a team beforehand. It is a planning aid, not a fixed zodiac lore rule.

## Endpoint card

| Field | Decision |
|---|---|
| Public system/value | What ordinary people depend on |
| Failure trigger | Why it fails now; separate documented lore from original invention |
| Deadline | A visible, causally meaningful time limit |
| Failure cost | Concrete harm to ordinary people and the world |
| Bottleneck count | Usually one indispensable bottleneck per major protagonist, but not necessarily one symbolic station per animal |
| Entry signal | What each protagonist independently notices |
| False public story | What the visible claimant says happened |
| Visibility gap | Why the public/record believes the claimant |
| Second failure | What the claimant cannot repair because they know only the endpoint |
| Evidence chain | Material traces, time records, witnesses, unique tools, causal reconstruction |
| Public settlement | Who sees the proof and what status changes |
| Next-season dispute | Why recognition does not automatically equal formal office |

## Reliable emotional sequence

1. Independent local motives respond to one shared crisis.
2. Partial information makes protagonists interfere with one another.
3. One protagonist is misunderstood; another's labor is not witnessed.
4. The outside claimant performs or reaches the visible final step.
5. Public records compress distributed labor into the claimant's story.
6. False victory gives the claimant status and forces the protagonists to absorb a social loss.
7. The public system fails again because the claimant lacks the full causal model.
8. The protagonists repair the harm before defending their names.
9. Independent evidence reconstructs the chain in public.
10. The season debt is settled, while a fair but unresolved institutional dispute launches the next season.

## Design cautions

- Do not make the claimant the only villain if the real mechanism is a visibility or record-keeping gap; give them a believable motive such as recognition hunger, status anxiety, or access to official reporting.
- Do not resolve misrecognition with one confession, a magical recording, or a suddenly appearing authority. Use several evidence lines with different functions.
- Do not let the team become emotionally unified before the first collision. Trust should be earned by observing consequences and choosing the public good under pressure.
- Do not make “helping” characters incompetent by default. Some should be sincere but mismatched, some indifferent, some harmful through status-seeking, and some effective only in a less visible role.
- Do not lock episode count until the deadline, failure cost, bottleneck order, false victory, second failure, and public settlement are stable.

## Working example pattern

A fictional public seasonal infrastructure fails before a new-year transition. Twelve protagonists independently respond to diagnosis, structural support, access, transport, fabrication, local cooperation, logistics, timing, and safety bottlenecks. A persuasive outsider reaches the final control point and receives credit. The system fails again because the outsider knows only the visible endpoint. The protagonists return to save ordinary people, then prove their distributed labor through physical traces, synchronized records, witnesses, and causal reconstruction. Public recognition leads naturally to a later contest for formal office rather than ending the story immediately.
